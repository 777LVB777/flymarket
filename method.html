Section 1

The method

This page explains how a market is shown to the fly circuit, how the circuit makes a choice, and how it learns from the result.

1.1

The four steps

Step 1

The market

Each market is described by three numbers: its current price, how much the price recently changed, and how long until it closes. Each number is converted into a firing rate between 30 and 150 spikes per second.

Step 2

The input

Those firing rates drive small groups of Kenyon cells, the main input neurons of the mushroom body. Each group has 100 cells, all on the left side of the brain. The YES side and the NO side of a market are shown separately.

Step 3

The readout

The circuit's response is read from 96 mushroom-body output neurons. Each output neuron is scored according to the type of dopamine neuron that serves its region.

Step 4

The choice

Whichever side, YES or NO, produces the higher score is the circuit's pick. If the two scores are too close, the circuit abstains and nothing is learned from that market.

1.2

The learning rule

When a market closes, its outcome becomes a teaching signal. The rule then weakens the connections from the Kenyon cells that were active to the output neurons in the regions that match that signal. This mirrors how learning is thought to work in the real fly mushroom body. Over time, all connections slowly drift back toward the strengths in the original wiring map.

The learning rule is ours. The brain model from Shiu et al. has no learning of its own. The teaching signal is applied directly by the rule; the model's dopamine neurons are not used to deliver it.

1.3

What comes from where

The brain model
A spiking model of the adult fly central brain by Shiu et al. (2024, Nature), using FlyWire wiring version 783. We use it unchanged.
The learning rule
Written by us for this project, as described above.
The input groups
Left-side only. Groups drawn from both sides of the brain caused the whole mushroom body to fire at once (see the results).
The markets
Simulated markets first, then historical Polymarket markets replayed offline. There is no live trading and no real money.
The rules
Each test's pass and fail criteria were written and published before the test was run.
