3.1

The scoring

The circuit is trained on one set of markets and then tested on markets it has never seen. On each test market it picks YES, picks NO, or abstains. A correct pick is a win, a wrong pick is a loss, and an abstention earns and loses nothing. The dates on this page are the dates the circuit made each decision, between March 8 and August 31, 2026; the whole test is replayed offline.

The circuit passes only if its Brier score (a measure of forecast error; lower is better) beats both the frozen training-calibrated price and its own learning-off version, with the 95 percent lower bounds of both improvements above zero. A third version, trained on randomly shuffled outcomes, is a control: it must not beat the frozen training-calibrated price. If it does, the verdict is null control failed: a pipeline problem, not a finding, and no edge is reported even if the circuit passes. These rules were fixed in advance. Only daily totals are published here, never individual markets or prices.

The profit and loss shown on this page is reported, not tested. The verdict depends only on the Brier score comparisons above, so a rising or falling P&L line on its own does not mean the circuit passed or failed.

The price-only line on this page needs a trading rule, which the preregistration does not define, because the preregistered comparison uses the forecast itself. For display, the price-only trader buys YES when its forecast is above the market price and NO when it is below, the same zero margin the circuit uses. This rule was chosen on October 18, 2026, before any real-market result existed, and it is not part of the pass rule.
